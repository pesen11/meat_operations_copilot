# Receiving & Vendor Management SOP — Meat Cutting Department

> **Note on this document:** Synthetic example SOP built for the Operations
> Copilot project, modeled on general industry receiving practice. It is not
> derived from a real employer's procedures and is not verified regulatory
> guidance. It deliberately shares vocabulary ("temperature", "log",
> "reject", "cooler") with `food_safety_sop.md` so that retrieval evaluation
> has realistic near-miss chunks to discriminate between.

## 1. Purpose

This SOP covers what happens between a delivery truck arriving and a primal
being available to cut: inspection, acceptance or rejection, put-away, and
what to do when the vendor sends the wrong thing or nothing at all.

Food safety requirements for incoming product live in the Food Safety SOP,
Section 3. This document covers the **commercial and operational** side:
counts, weights, substitutions, short shipments, and escalation.

## 2. Scope

Applies to every delivery of raw primal or sub-primal beef, pork, and lamb
into the cutting department. Fish and poultry receiving follows the same
temperature rules but is handled by the fishmonger and is out of scope here.

## 3. Delivery schedule

| Day | Delivery expected | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Monday–Saturday | Yes | Standard vendor delivery window is 05:00–07:30 |
| Sunday | No | No deliveries; Sunday cutting runs off Saturday's stock |

Because there is no Sunday delivery, Saturday's receiving must cover both
Saturday and Sunday cutting. Check the Sunday production plan before
accepting a short shipment on a Saturday — a shortfall that would be a
half-day problem midweek is a two-day problem on a Saturday.

## 4. Inspection at the dock

Do all of the following **before** the driver leaves. A signature on the
manifest is an acceptance of what is written on it.

1. **Temperature.** Probe at least one box per pallet between (not through)
   the packaging. Product must be at or below 4°C. See Food Safety SOP
   Section 3 for the full requirement and the rejection rule.
2. **Case count.** Count boxes against the manifest. Do not accept a
   manifest count "to be corrected later".
3. **Weight.** Spot-weigh at least one box per primal type. Catch-weight
   product varies, but a box more than 10% off its expected weight is a
   discrepancy, not natural variation.
4. **Packaging integrity.** Reject any vacuum-packed primal with a lost
   seal, standing purge, or a punctured bag.
5. **Labels and dates.** Confirm the pack date and the vendor lot code are
   legible on every box. An illegible lot code breaks traceability and the
   box must be rejected — see Food Safety SOP, labeling and traceability.

## 6. Discrepancy handling

A discrepancy is anything where what arrived does not match what was
ordered. Handle by type:

**Short shipment (fewer boxes than ordered).**
1. Note the shortfall on the manifest before signing and have the driver
   initial it.
2. Record the shortfall in the receiving log with the primal, the number of
   boxes short, and the vendor lot.
3. Check the day's production plan. If the shortfall puts a primal below one
   day of cutting coverage, tell the department lead immediately — do not
   wait for the morning cut to discover it.

**Substitution (vendor sent a different primal or grade).**
1. Do not accept a substitution for a graded product (Wagyu, Prime) without
   the department lead's approval. Grade affects both the cutting spec and
   the price the product is sold at.
2. An ungraded like-for-like substitution of the same primal at the same
   spec may be accepted, but must be logged so the yield variance is
   explainable later.

**Over-shipment.** Accept only what can be stored below 4°C and cut within
the product's normal turn. Refusing surplus is better than holding product
past its useful life; log the refusal.

**Quality rejection.** Product that fails inspection is refused at the dock
and goes back on the truck. Do not accept it "pending credit" — once it is
in the cooler it is the department's problem.

## 7. Vendor stockout

A vendor stockout is when the vendor cannot supply a primal at all, usually
notified the night before or discovered at the dock.

1. Log the stockout with the primal, the date, and the expected recovery
   date. Stockouts typically resolve within three to four delivery days.
2. Check coverage: divide boxes on hand by the primal's normal daily draw.
   Under two days of coverage, escalate to the department lead the same
   morning.
3. Consider a cut-plan change rather than an empty case. A primal that
   feeds two products (Blade Eyes feeds both chuck roast and thinly sliced
   blades) can be re-routed toward the faster-moving product for the
   duration of the stockout — this is a lead's decision, not a cutter's.
4. Do **not** substitute a different primal into a product's spec to fill a
   gap. A chuck roast cut from something that is not Blade Eyes is
   mislabeled product, not a substitution.

## 8. Put-away

1. Move accepted product to the cooler within 15 minutes of acceptance.
2. Rotate FIFO: new boxes go behind existing stock of the same primal, never
   in front.
3. Record boxes received against the primal in the stock count so the
   morning coverage figure is accurate before the first cut of the day.
4. Aged product (dry or wet aging programs) is segregated on its own rack
   with its aging start date visible. Do not put a fresh box on an aging
   rack because there was space.

## 9. Receiving log

Every delivery gets one log entry, kept for 12 months, recording:

- Date and time of delivery
- Vendor and driver name
- Primal, box count, and spot-check weight per line
- Probe temperature and where it was taken
- Any discrepancy, rejection, or substitution, with the reason
- Receiver's initials

The log is the only evidence available when a yield variance or a customer
complaint is traced back weeks later. An entry written from memory at the
end of a shift is worth very little; write it at the dock.
