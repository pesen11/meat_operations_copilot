# Equipment Cleaning & Maintenance SOP — Meat Cutting Department

> **Note on this document:** Synthetic example SOP for the Operations
> Copilot project. Chemical concentrations, torque figures, and service
> intervals below are illustrative, not verified manufacturer or regulatory
> specifications. A real operation must follow the equipment manufacturer's
> manual and its own verified sanitation plan.
>
> This document overlaps deliberately with `food_safety_sop.md` on the words
> "clean", "sanitize", and "contamination". The two answer different
> questions — food safety covers *why and how clean*, this document covers
> *which machine, how often, and by whom* — and distinguishing them is a
> genuine retrieval test.

## 1. Purpose

Defines the cleaning, sanitation, and preventive-maintenance routine for
every powered and hand tool in the cutting department, and who is
responsible for each.

## 2. Scope and responsibility

| Equipment | Daily clean | Deep clean | Maintenance owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Band saw | Closing cutter | Weekly (Sunday close) | Department lead |
| Grinder | Operator, after each use | Daily (end of grind runs) | Department lead |
| Slicer | Operator, after each use | Weekly | Department lead |
| Vacuum sealer | Closing wrapper | Weekly | Department lead |
| Cutting tables | Every cutter, after each species change | Daily close | All staff |
| Knives and steels | Each user, continuously | Daily close | Each user |
| Tote bins and lugs | Wash-up | Daily | Wash-up |

No cutter services their own machine beyond blade changes and daily
cleaning. Anything requiring a tool, a panel removal, or an adjustment goes
to the department lead.

## 3. Lockout before cleaning

**Every powered machine is unplugged before any cleaning begins.** Not
switched off — unplugged, with the cord visible to the person cleaning.

1. Power down at the machine's own switch.
2. Unplug at the wall and lay the cord over the machine body.
3. Only then remove guards, blades, or plates.
4. Reassemble fully before plugging back in. A machine is never left
   part-assembled at shift change; either finish it or tag it out of service.

Any machine tagged out of service stays tagged until the department lead
clears it. Removing someone else's out-of-service tag is a disciplinary
matter, not a judgement call.

## 4. Band saw

**Daily (closing):**
1. Unplug. Remove the blade, upper and lower wheel covers, table insert, and
   meat pusher.
2. Scrape bone dust and fat from the wheels, wheel tires, and blade guides.
   Bone dust packed into a wheel tire throws the blade off track the next
   morning.
3. Wash all removed parts in the sink at 45–50°C with detergent, rinse, then
   sanitize (see Section 8 for concentrations).
4. Wash the frame and table in place. Do not hose the motor housing.
5. Air-dry fully before reassembly. Reassembling wet invites rust on the
   blade guides and leaves standing water on food-contact surfaces.

**Weekly (Sunday close):** as daily, plus remove and clean the blade
tensioner and check the wheel tires for cracking or embedded bone chips.

**Blade changes:**
- Change the blade when it wanders, requires noticeably more push pressure,
  or leaves a rough cut surface — typically every 2–3 cutting days on
  bone-in work, longer on boneless.
- Blades are changed by a trained cutter only, machine unplugged, using cut
  gloves.
- Used blades go directly into the blade disposal box. Never into the
  general waste bin.

## 5. Grinder

**After each use:**
1. Unplug. Remove the auger, knife, plate, and ring.
2. Push out residual product — do not run the machine empty to clear it.
3. Wash, rinse, sanitize, air-dry.
4. Inspect the knife and plate as a matched pair. A dull or mismatched
   knife-and-plate pair smears fat instead of cutting it, which shows up as
   a greasy, pasty grind and pushes grind temperature up.

**Daily:** strip the head completely, including the neck and hopper, and
clean the machine body.

**Between species:** a full strip-and-sanitize is required between grinding
beef and grinding pork — not a wipe-down. See Food Safety SOP for the
species-separation rule this implements.

## 6. Slicer

**After each use:**
1. Unplug. Set the gauge plate to zero.
2. Remove the carriage, product table, and blade guard.
3. Clean the blade **from the centre outwards**, with a cloth, never a
   scraper, and never toward the edge.
4. Wash, rinse, sanitize removed parts; wipe down the body and the slide rod.
5. Reassemble; leave the gauge plate at zero.

**Weekly:** remove the ring guard and clean behind it. This is where product
accumulates unseen and is the most common slicer sanitation failure.

**Sharpening:** on the machine's own sharpener, monthly or when the blade
stops producing a clean edge. Run the sharpening cycle, then clean the blade
again — sharpening leaves metal fines on the blade.

## 7. Vacuum sealer

**Daily (closing):** wipe the chamber, the seal bar, and the silicone
counter-bar. Check the seal bar's PTFE tape for burn-through and replace it
if the seal is inconsistent.

**Weekly:** check and, if the sight glass shows it clouded or above the
fill line, change the pump oil. Cloudy oil means moisture ingress and a
falling vacuum level, which shows up as short shelf life on vacuum-packed
primals well before anyone suspects the sealer.

## 8. Cleaning chemicals and concentrations

| Use | Chemical | Concentration | Contact time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Detergent wash | Food-grade alkaline detergent | Per label | — |
| Sanitizing (food contact) | Chlorine | 100–200 ppm | 60 seconds, no-rinse |
| Sanitizing (food contact, alt.) | Quaternary ammonium | 200 ppm | 60 seconds, no-rinse |
| Floors and drains | Chlorinated foam cleaner | Per label | Per label |

Rules that get broken most often, and matter most:

- **Wash before sanitize, always.** Sanitizer applied to a soiled surface is
  neutralized by the soil and does nothing.
- **Verify concentration with a test strip**, not by eye or by pour count.
  Log the reading at each shift's close.
- **Never mix chlorine with any acid cleaner.** This releases chlorine gas.
- Sanitizer solution is made fresh each shift. Yesterday's bucket is not
  sanitizer.

## 9. Preventive maintenance schedule

| Interval | Task |
|---|---|
| Daily | Visual check of cords, plugs, and guards on every machine before first use |
| Weekly | Band saw wheel tires; slicer ring guard; sealer PTFE tape |
| Monthly | Slicer blade sharpening; grinder knife/plate pairing check; scale calibration check |
| Quarterly | Vacuum pump oil change; band saw wheel alignment and tracking |
| Annually | Manufacturer service on band saw, grinder, slicer, and sealer |

## 10. Breakdown and escalation

1. Tag the machine out of service and unplug it.
2. Tell the department lead immediately — before starting a workaround.
3. Record the failure in the maintenance log: machine, time, what happened,
   and what was being processed.
4. Do not improvise a repair on a food-contact machine. A machine that has
   been opened for repair gets a full wash, rinse, and sanitize before it
   returns to service, whether or not it was touched internally.

## 11. Maintenance log

One entry per event, kept for 24 months: date, machine, task or failure,
parts changed, chemical concentration readings where relevant, and initials.
The log is what turns a repeated failure into a warranty claim or a
replacement decision instead of an ongoing annoyance nobody can quantify.
